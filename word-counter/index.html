What counts as a word?

A word is any sequence of non-whitespace characters separated by spaces, tabs, or newlines.

Reading time

Calculated at 200 words per minute (approx. average reading speed). Useful for estimating blog post, speech, or essay read times.

Why Word Count Still Matters

Word and character limits show up everywhere: essay assignments with a minimum word count, meta descriptions capped at roughly 155-160 characters, tweets and social posts with hard character limits, cover letters expected to stay under a page, and academic abstracts with strict word ceilings. Manually counting any of these is slow and error-prone, especially once a document runs past a paragraph or two. This tool counts as you type, so you can see exactly where you stand without stopping to select text and check a word processor's status bar.

What Each Metric Actually Measures

  • Words — counted as whitespace-separated chunks of text. Hyphenated terms like "well-known" count as one word; "New York" counts as two.
  • Characters — every character including spaces and punctuation. This is the number most relevant to SEO meta titles/descriptions and SMS/character-capped fields.
  • Characters (no spaces) — useful when a platform's limit specifically excludes whitespace from its count, which some do.
  • Sentences — approximated by splitting on sentence-ending punctuation (., !, ?). Abbreviations like "e.g." or "Dr." can occasionally inflate this count slightly, which is normal for any automated sentence counter.
  • Paragraphs — counted as blocks of text separated by line breaks.
  • Reading time — estimated at roughly 200 words per minute, the commonly cited average adult silent-reading speed, so you can gauge how long a blog post, script, or speech will take to read or deliver.

Common Uses

Writers use word counters to hit publication or contest word limits. Students use them to verify essay length requirements before submission. Marketers use the character count to keep meta descriptions, ad copy, and social captions within platform limits. Speechwriters use the reading-time estimate to time a script to a target duration without rehearsing it out loud first.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does pasting formatted text (from Word or Google Docs) affect the count?

The tool counts plain text only — formatting like bold or italics doesn't add to the count, but hidden characters from some editors can occasionally add extra whitespace. If a count looks off, try pasting into a plain text editor first.

Is there a limit to how much text I can paste?

No hard limit is enforced, but very large documents (tens of thousands of words) may count with a slight delay since counting happens live as you type.

Does this tool save or upload my text?

No. Counting happens entirely in your browser with JavaScript — your text is never sent anywhere or stored after you close the tab.
